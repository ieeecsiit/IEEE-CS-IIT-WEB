Ethical AI: Balancing Innovation with Responsibility

‍

By Deshan Goonasekara

‍

Overview

On the 4th of April, during Microsoft's 50th Anniversary event, Ibtihal Aboussad, a Microsoft employee, protested the company, regarding how Microsoft profits from selling its AI technology to the Israeli military.

‍

“Shame on you, you claim that you care about using AI for good, but Microsoft sells AI weapons to the Israeli military. Fifty-thousand people have died and Microsoft powers this genocide in our region.” She said before being escorted out of the event.
This is one of the examples on how it showcases how important it is to consider ethical implications of AI to balance innovation alongside with responsibility.

‍

AI has become synonymous with tech moving from the latter of 2022, and it’s becoming more difficult to escape from it as it encompasses to everything within our day to day lives. However, it's also more important than ever to also consider how to balance ethics whilst powering innovation as we move forward with AI.

‍

1.Liability Concerns

Who should be responsible if a machine does a mistake? What if an error in a machine caused the company to lose revenue, or worse, harm a life of an individual? In 2024, a customer in AirCanada got mislead by its chatbot for a bereavement fare that it could apply after the booking, something which AirCanada didn't offer, and the customer ended up suing the airline in court. AirCanada tried to get out of the situation by arguing AI can make mistakes. However, the judge ruled in favour for the customer, quoting “If you are handing over part of your business to AI, you are responsible for what it does”. Microsoft providing AI systems for the Israeli military is also another example on how AI can harm individuals if profit comes before ethics. The Chinese government also spies on their everyday citizens for both crime and authoritarianism. Therefore, responsibility should be carried out by the companies who deploy and innovate in such systems, without harming lives of individuals.
